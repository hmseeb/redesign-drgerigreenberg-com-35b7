Seasonal

Autumn the Season of the Lung

A drying, simplifying season that asks you to protect your immunity.

Read

Autumn is contractive: eliminating what is unnecessary and storing only what is needed. Weakness of the Lung brings respiratory trouble, frequent colds, lowered immunity, sinus headaches, skin ailments and dryness. Pungent foods open the lung energy, while cooked pears, apples and squashes moisten a dry season.

Acupuncture & herbs

Balancing Emotional Health with Acupuncture & Chinese Herbs

Each meridian carries an emotion — and responds when it is out of balance.

Read

When qi in the meridians goes out of balance, some people experience anxiety, worry or other difficult emotional states. Liver imbalance can bring irritability and frustration; Heart imbalance anxiety and insomnia; Spleen excessive worry; Lung prolonged grief; Kidney fear and loss of will. Acupuncture with specific Chinese herbal formulas helps restore optimal qi flow and balance emotional health.

Foods that Lower Cholesterol

Everyday foods that do quiet work for your heart.

Read

Dr. Geri’s food-first list for patients watching their cholesterol: oats and barley, beans and lentils, nuts, olive oil, apples, citrus, aubergine and okra, soy foods and oily fish. Simple swaps, kept up over a season, do more than any single change.

Top 15 Anti-inflammatory foods

What to put on the plate when something hurts.

Read

Berries, leafy greens, broccoli, oily fish, olive oil, avocado, turmeric, ginger, green tea, nuts, cherries, beets, tomatoes, mushrooms and dark chocolate all help quiet inflammation — useful alongside Chiropractic and Acupuncture when you’re healing from an injury.

Stress & sleep

Natural Ways to Calm Stress

Reducing stress through the five senses, one small habit at a time.

Read

Look into the distance instead of at a screen; listen to soothing music or your own breath; eat calming foods such as berries, bananas and chamomile tea; massage your ears; use lavender on your pillow and mint or grapefruit in the car. Acupuncture and Acupressure release the muscle tension underneath it all.

Nutrients & Easy Brain boosting techniques

Food, sleep and movement all sharpen clear thinking.

Read

Oily fish, walnuts, blueberries, leafy greens, eggs and green tea support memory and focus. Pair them with daily movement, real sleep and rosemary — which Dr. Geri recommends for mood and memory.

Seasonal

Summer the Season of Yang

Fire ignites all of life — keep the heart cool and steady.

Read

When the Fire element is balanced the heart is strong, the mind calm and sleep sound. Cooling yin foods help: watermelon, cantaloupe, citrus, peaches, cucumber, asparagus, broccoli, bok choy, spinach and Asian pears. Avoid heavy outdoor activity from 10 am to 2 pm.

Cramping is usually a signal about minerals, water or circulation.

Winter the Season of the Kidney

Cold and darkness turn us inward, towards warmth and rest.

Read

The kidney meridian governs immune support, will, sharp thinking and fluid metabolism, and influences the bones, hair and ears. Nourish it with warm hearty soups, whole grains, roasted nuts, black beans and root vegetables.

Jaw pain often starts in the neck, scalp and shoulders.

Do your energy levels go up and down?

The Meridian Clock shows which meridians may need balancing.

Read

Each meridian comes into its highest action at a certain time of day. So if you notice fatigue at a particular hour, or wake at a specific time and can’t get back to sleep, those are indicators of imbalance in those meridians. Acupuncture with Chinese herbs helps tonify and balance them.

Breathing & Calming the mind

Focused on the breath, the whole body and mind quiet down.

Read

Pain & posture

Incorrect vs. Correct head posture for cellphone use

Every inch your head drops forward multiplies the load on your neck.

Read

Raise the phone towards eye level instead of dropping your chin to it, keep your shoulders back, and take breaks to look into the distance. Sustained forward head posture is one of the most common causes of neck pain, muscle tension and headaches Dr. Geri sees.

Seasonal

Spring — the Season of the Liver

Growth, flexibility and the smooth flow of plans and decisions.

Read

Spring governs the liver and gallbladder meridians. Imbalance can show up as spinal problems, poor flexibility, arthritis, headache, neck pain, allergies, eye problems and indigestion. Green foods nourish it: cooked zucchini, asparagus, string beans, celery, Swiss chard, soups, berries, baked apples and mint tea.
