What is stress?

Recognising it early changes your quality of life

Stress is the body’s reaction to pressure, tension, and change. Recognizing the early signs of stress and learning to deal with them can make an important difference in the quality of life. Life without stress can be dull and uneventful. Life with too much stress can be debilitating and distressful.

Signs of stress

Cold hands, rapid breathing, rapid heartbeat, headaches, neck and low back pain, muscle tension, spasm, poor sleep, and decreased immunity leading to susceptibility to illness.

Acupuncture, Acupressure and Chiropractic release muscle tension, blocked circulation and qi/energy flow, thereby harmonizing and balancing the body and emotions, creating a deep sense of calm… naturally.

Between sessions

Reducing stress through the five senses

Simple, natural practices Dr. Geri recommends to her patients — walking, swimming, yoga, Tai chi, meditation and short naps, plus slow hobbies like gardening, painting, music, photography, making sand castles and reading.

Seeing

When we look into the distance instead of at our computers or phones, we balance the eye muscles and at the same time “get out of our heads” — relaxing the mind and clearing both inner and outer vision.

Hearing

Listening to soothing music, the sounds of nature, or our own deep breaths in silence naturally calms us. Dr. Geri recommends Mindfulness, Meditation by Jon Kabat-Zinn, and the soothing Canyon Trilogy by R. Carlos Nakai — the music you hear in the office.

Taste

Certain foods and herbs are naturally calming: berries, bananas and apples to name a few, along with chamomile and mint tea.

Touch

The entire body is represented on the ears, so massaging the ears is an easy way to relax wherever you happen to be.

Smell

Our sense of smell is 10,000 times more sensitive than our other senses. Lavender calms the mind and anxiety; grapefruit and mint clear frustration, lift our spirits and help us re-focus. Put 6–10 drops of essential oil in a spray bottle with 6 oz of water, then spritz lavender on your pillow, or mint or grapefruit in the air at work or in the car.

Breathing

Find a comfortable position and consciously soften any obvious tension. Bring your attention to the sensations of your breathing — coolness in the nostrils, movement of the chest, the rise and fall of the belly. When your mind wanders, gently bring your attention back to the next breath. Focused on the breath, the whole body and mind quiet down.
