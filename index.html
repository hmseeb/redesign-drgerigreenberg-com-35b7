Before you agree to surgery for low back pain

Research on MRI examination of the lumbar spine has found that many people without back pain have disc bulges. Given this, a disc bulge in someone with low back pain may frequently be a coincidental finding — so conservative care may be recommended for initial treatment. Even a lot of back pain does not by itself mean surgery is warranted; surgery has been found to be helpful in only 1 in 100 cases.

That study also found degenerative disc changes are part of the normal aging process rather than a pathological process requiring surgical intervention. According to the Mayo Clinic, most disc changes are no more significant than gray hair or crow’s feet. Before medications, shots or surgery, it is worth seeking out Chiropractic or Acupuncture care.

Seasonal tune-ups

Living in harmony with the seasons

The ancient Chinese observed the seasons, their cyclical climatic changes, and the benefits of living in harmony with them. Each season corresponds to an element and a pair of meridians — and each one responds to its own seasonal Acupuncture and Chiropractic tune-up.

Spring · Wood

The Season of the Liver

Spring governs the liver and gallbladder meridians — growth, flexibility, and the smooth flow of plans and decisions. Imbalance can show up as spinal problems, poor flexibility, arthritis, headache, neck pain, allergies, eye problems and indigestion. Green foods nourish it: cooked zucchini, asparagus, string beans, celery, Swiss chard, soups, berries, baked apples and mint tea.

Summer · Fire

The Season of Yang

Fire ignites all of life — activity, warmth and connection. When the fire element is balanced the heart is strong, the mind calm and sleep sound. Cooling yin foods help: watermelon, cantaloupe, citrus, peaches, cucumber, asparagus, broccoli, bok choy, spinach and Asian pears. Avoid heavy outdoor activity from 10 am to 2 pm.

Autumn · Harvest

The Season of the Lung

A contractive, simplifying time — eliminating what is unnecessary and storing only what is needed. Weakness of the Lung brings respiratory trouble, frequent colds, lowered immunity, sinus headaches, skin ailments and dryness. Pungent foods open the lung energy; cooked pears, apples and squashes moisten a dry season.

Winter · Water

The Season of the Kidney

Cold and darkness drive us to seek inner warmth, reflection and rest. The kidney meridian governs immune support, will, sharp thinking and fluid metabolism, and influences the bones, hair and ears. Nourish it with warm hearty soups, whole grains, roasted nuts, black beans and root vegetables.

Chinese Herbology

Herbs that keep working between visits

The combination of Acupuncture and specific Chinese Herbal Formulas helps restore optimal qi/energy flow and balance emotional health. Formulas are chosen for your constitution, your pattern and the time of year — warming formulas in winter, liver-harmonizing formulas in spring, lung-supporting formulas in autumn.

Everyday herbs and spices matter too. Ginger steeped with lemon and honey helps with cold and flu prevention and relieves muscular aches brought on by exercise. Turmeric reduces pain and speeds healing. Mint eases digestion and moves qi when you’re feeling stuck. Rosemary boosts mood and memory. Thyme relaxes the muscles of the trachea and bronchi.
